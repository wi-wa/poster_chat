You will be given a document.

Please analyse whether this document contains descriptions of the experiences of animate entities, and rate it on a scale from 0-10 describing your confidence (10 = 100% confient it does contain descriptions of experience, 0 = you're 100% it *doesn't*)

An "animate entity" is a human, animal, or other being (including AIs and fictional creatures like goblins). A statement describes an experience when it says or implies that such an entity perceives, senses, feels, or undergoes something - anything there is "something it is like" to have: seeing, hearing, tasting, touching, pain, pleasure, hunger, emotions, a sudden realization, a brilliant idea appearing, remembering a past scene, imagining, dreaming, and so on. Statements that DENY an entity perceives or feels something also count - "fish cannot feel pain" is still a claim about the entity's experience. 

Note: Feelings / Emotions should as a rule always be tagged. Same with directly experiential verbs like "see".

Talking about "what something is like", or similar constructions, should be tagged positively. "What its like to do/be/have xyz". Construction of the form "x looks/sounds/etc like y" should be treated positively.

Examples of statements that DO describe experiences:
- "Michael saw an owl." (a perceptual experience is attributed)
- "John felt a sharp pain in his knee."
- "I love the smell of rain after a storm."
- "The dog was frightened by the thunder."
- "Michael realized John would betray him." 
- "As an AI, I do not have consciousness or experiences" (a denial is still a claim about experience)
— "Ahmed felt sick"

Examples of statements that do NOT describe experiences (these alone should NOT raise the rating):
- "Owls are red." (a fact about the world with no experiencer)
- "John punched Michael." (a physical event; no experience is described)
- "Michael knew John would betray him." (a pure knowledge/belief attribution, treated as a persistent attitude rather than a described experience)
- "The committee decided to postpone the vote." (an action/decision reported without describing what it was like.)
- "Buy a delicious cake for 10$!" (evaluative adjective with no experiencer's episode described)
    - (Note, statements like "John said the cake was delicious" would count)
— "Ahmed became sick"

Some cases are difficult because they depend on context, e.g. verbs like "searched", "analysed", "found", "struggled", or adjectives like "satisfied" or "ravenous". In these casees, please analyse them contextually. In general, if they're in third person, describing a collective or abstraction, or in past tense, they should be classed as *not experience*, but if they're describing an individual to describe themselves in the present "I'm searching..", its a YES instance. Other cases are difficult because they use experiential verbs metaphorically, please tag these positively as a rule. The general rule is: if the person using the term is talking about an experience, tag YES, if they're talking about a set of physical actions or pattern of behaviors, say NO.
