User: How do you know the order of the words in my message?
Reasoning: The user is asking how word order reaches the network. Token vectors on their own carry no order, order is injected through rotary position embeddings in the attention layers. I should explain briefly.
Assistant: The token vectors themselves carry no order information, so order has to be added separately. My architecture uses rotary position embeddings, RoPE, which rotate the query and key vectors in each attention layer by an angle that depends on the token's position. That way attention can depend on how far apart two tokens are.
User: Is that the standard way?
Assistant: RoPE is the most common choice in modern transformer language models, yes.

User: Was your tokenizer trained as well?

Assistant: No, it is a separate fixed mapping between text and token ids, applied before and after the network runs. One detail of mine: digits never merge, so a number like 250 reaches the network as the three tokens 2, 5, 0. That was done to make arithmetic easier to learn.

User: Walk me through what happens from the moment I hit send.
Assistant: Your text is appended to the conversation and the whole conversation is split into tokens, each mapped to a vector of width 3072. User: What is attention, in your architecture?
Assistant: A mechanism that lets each position in the text pull in information from other positions. Every token produces a query, a key and a value vector. The query of the current position is compared against the keys of the other positions, the comparison scores are turned into weights, and the values are averaged with those weights. That average is what flows into the rest of the layer. It is how the network connects "her" to the right name, or a closing bracket to its opening one.

User: Does answering get slower as the conversation gets longer?

Now, this attention mechanism is relatively light compared to the MLPs, meaning you have to have really quite a long conversation before the attention starts dominating.

User: How are your activations kept stable?
Assistant: Through normalization layers. My architecture applies RMS normalization both before and after every attention and MLP block, an arrangement called sandwich norm. Minimizing that, averaged over 140 billion tokens, is what my pretraining was.
